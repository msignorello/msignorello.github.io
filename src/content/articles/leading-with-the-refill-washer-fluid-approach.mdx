To me, this is one of the most universally relatable messages that a car owner can experience (aside from the dreaded check engine light...). Why do most of us ignore it until it's too late?

## A warning without a crisis

It's a gentle nudge that doesn't demand immediate action but serves as a reminder that attention is needed. As leaders, we can learn a lot from this seemingly annoying alert.

Procrastination often gets a bad rap, but what if we viewed it as a tool for effective leadership?

Just like that dashboard warning, procrastination allows us to pause and gather our thoughts, rather than rushing into action.

For example: Where is the best place to get this fluid? When can I put it in? Where is the filler located? Go for the cheap blue stuff or the orange juice?

This period of reflection is crucial. It provides the space to establish a clear plan, ensuring that when we do act, our actions are deliberate and well-considered.

## The useful side of waiting

In leading strong teams, embracing this approach can be transformative. Here’s why:

- **Thought gathering:** Procrastination allows us and our teams to collect our thoughts. This breathing space is essential for innovative thinking and problem-solving.
- **Planning time:** It helps set a clear direction. By avoiding the rush, we can map out strategies that are both effective and efficient.
- **Empowerment:** When we delay immediate solutions, we give our teams the opportunity to think for themselves. This autonomy fosters a sense of ownership and accountability.
- **Avoiding micromanagement:** As leaders, it’s easy to jump in and solve every problem. Stepping back and allowing your team to navigate challenges can lead to more creative and robust solutions.

## Know when the pause is over

However, it’s important to recognize that procrastination has its limits. Just as neglecting to refill washer fluid can leave you hoping that the person in front of you shares some "mist", waiting too long to address critical issues can lead to setbacks. Knowing when to step in is crucial to avoid failure.

By promoting empathy, transparency, and active engagement, we can harness the power of procrastination wisely. It’s not about avoiding tasks but rather allowing time for thoughtful consideration and empowering our teams to shine while knowing the right moment to act.

> Waiting is useful only while it improves the decision.

<aside class="article-takeaways">
  <strong>The refill-washer-fluid test</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Pause when more context will improve the decision.</li>
    <li>Use the time to clarify ownership and direction.</li>
    <li>Let the team solve problems before stepping in.</li>
    <li>Act before a small warning becomes a failure.</li>
  </ul>
</aside>

Next time you see that "Refill Washer Fluid" alert, remember: sometimes, waiting a bit before acting can lead to the best outcomes, but knowing when to act is key to avoiding potential failures.
