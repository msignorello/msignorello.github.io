Every leader faces challenging environments, but what do you do when the culture around you starts to conflict with your values? New boss, new CEO, new VP? From my own experience, I learned that to thrive—and maintain integrity—you need to approach the situation like a Jedi. Here are the key lessons I learned about navigating difficult leadership shifts and staying true to your mission, even when circumstances don’t align with your efforts.

## 1. Trust in the Force: stay true to your values

When leadership shifts occur, it can feel like the ground beneath you is shifting, too. The once open, collaborative environment might become less transparent. In these times, it’s tempting to adapt to the new dynamics for survival. But like a Jedi, true strength comes from trusting your values. Rather than chasing political moves or charm-based promotions, keep your focus on the long-term success of your team and the mission.

For me, empathy and collaboration grounded me, even as the culture leaned toward the Dark Side. (Like those charm-based promotions happening around you...)

<aside class="article-callout">
  <strong>The leadership test</strong>
  <p>A culture shift does not require a values shift. Adapt your tactics without abandoning the principles your team relies on.</p>
</aside>

## 2. Lead with empathy

Leadership is about people, not power. Even in turbulent environments, leading with empathy ensures your team feels supported. As the culture shifted, I embraced my Jedi mindset—resisting the urge to follow the changes blindly and focusing instead on my team. I listened, helped them manage uncertainty, and empowered them with the tools they needed to thrive, despite external chaos.

Empathy creates a space where everyone can flourish, even when adversity strikes.

## 3. Know when to resist, but stay professional

Sometimes, you have to resist—not out of defiance, but because it’s the right thing to do. The best example here is through repetitive re-organizational changes. I resisted changes that didn’t align with my team’s needs, not to protect myself, but to maintain the mission’s integrity. Like any Jedi, I focused on doing what was right, even if it meant going against the grain.

> Resistance isn’t about being difficult. It’s about voicing concerns constructively and staying professional.

## 4. Build relationships with integrity

Building authentic relationships is key. Much like a Jedi’s connection to the Force, the relationships I built were my foundation. While they strengthened my position, they also made me a target. Some, like the Emperor, feared that if I ever left, those connections would leave with me. It was a counterintuitive scenario: someone with that power should be able to maintain and build those same relationships, regardless of who started them.

Even in shifting environments, maintaining relationships with trust and integrity helps create bonds that outlast organizational changes. Just like Yoda and Obi-Wan being with Luke when he needed them.

## 5. Accept what you cannot control

Not everything is within your control. Leadership shifts often bring challenges to your style, and, like a Jedi facing a larger force, you need to focus on what you can control—your actions, team, and principles. I couldn’t stop the larger dynamics, but I never let them erode my commitment to my team’s success.

## 6. Understand that circumstances may prevail

Even when you do everything right—leading with empathy, resisting for the right reasons—circumstances may still win. Look at Obi-Wan when he was struck by Vader or Han Solo being frozen in carbonite.

Despite my efforts, I became a victim of circumstance. The shifting culture and power dynamics led to my dismissal—not because of my performance, but because I was seen as a threat in an environment no longer aligned with my values.

Sometimes, even a Jedi finds themselves caught in circumstances beyond their control.

<aside class="article-takeaways">
  <strong>What stays in your control</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Your values and professional conduct.</li>
    <li>How clearly you support your team.</li>
    <li>Which relationships you build with integrity.</li>
    <li>When you speak up and when you let go.</li>
  </ul>
</aside>

Being a Jedi in a leadership shift means staying true to your values, leading with empathy, and knowing when to resist without losing your professionalism. Good can prevail, just as the Emperor was eventually defeated and Han Solo was freed from the carbonite.

Leadership is about people, not power. And while you can’t always control cultural changes, staying grounded in your values is the true mark of a leader.
