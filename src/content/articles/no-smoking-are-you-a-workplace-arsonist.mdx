You know when you’re at the gas station, and you see that big “No Smoking” sign? It’s kind of ironic, right? Who needs to be told not to light up while pumping gas? But in the workplace, there’s a different kind of “gaslighting” going on—and it’s not just common sense that’s at risk.

This kind of gaslighting doesn’t involve a cigarette, cigar, or your favorite cannabis product. But it sure can make you feel like you’re standing next to an open flame and feeling the heat.

Picture this: you’re holding a book of matches or, better yet, a Bic lighter, and someone’s telling you it’s perfectly safe to strike one up at the pump. It’s a subtle form of psychological manipulation that leaves you questioning your reality. And when it’s coming from a boss, it’s like someone’s flicking that lighter dangerously close to your mental fuel tank—risky, disorienting, and definitely something to handle with care.

## When strengths are treated as threats

Even in high-performing teams, complex interpersonal challenges can arise. A leader might unintentionally contribute to a culture of subtle undermining, perhaps because of insecurity or miscommunication. High achievers are often known for:

- Strategic thinking
- Strong communication skills
- The ability to influence

Those strengths can be perceived as threats rather than assets. That misperception can lead to manipulation that chips away at confidence and causes people to second-guess their abilities and contributions.

## The impact of workplace manipulation

When someone is caught in this kind of manipulative environment, the consequences can be profound:

- **Erosion of self-confidence:** People begin to second-guess their ideas, focus, and instincts, wondering whether they are truly capable or have somehow been fooling themselves all along.
- **Trust issues:** That self-doubt extends to workplace relationships, making it difficult to trust the team, managers, and, most importantly, themselves.
- **Distorted decision-making:** Leaders swayed by skewed perceptions can end up making calls that are, let’s say, less than stellar.
- **Productivity slump:** The whole team can feel the effects as creativity and collaboration take a nosedive.

  <p>“Am I crazy, or is something really off here?” When capable people repeatedly ask themselves that question, leaders should pay attention.</p>

But here’s the thing: this kind of manipulation can be as unintentional as it is harmful. That’s why it’s so important to be self-aware—both to avoid becoming a “workplace arsonist” and to recognize when someone else is striking a match near you.

## How to prevent and address it

The good news is that strong, savvy leadership can nip this kind of thing in the bud. And it starts with a few key ingredients:

- **Emotional intelligence:** The ability to read the room, and yourself, goes a long way. If you are in a position of influence, check your motivations: are you fostering growth or inadvertently creating friction?
- **Transparency:** Keep the lines of communication open so everyone is working from the same information. Encourage a culture where concerns can be raised without fear of retribution.
- **Diverse perspectives:** Make sure all voices are heard, not just the loudest ones. A well-rounded view of team dynamics can prevent misunderstandings that lead to manipulation.

For anyone who’s been around the block a few times, spotting these subtle forms of manipulation becomes second nature. The trick is knowing what to do when you see it:

  <strong>What to do when something feels off</strong>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Stay sharp:</strong> Keep your radar tuned to signs that something is off.</li>
    <li><strong>Get backup:</strong> Loop in HR, a mentor, or a trusted colleague.</li>
    <li><strong>Keep it professional:</strong> Address the issue directly with a focus on finding solutions.</li>
  </ul>

At the end of the day, we’re all better off in workplaces where transparency, respect, and a good dose of mutual support are the norms. So, before you reach for that lighter, ask yourself—are you sparking creativity, or could you be a workplace arsonist? Because nobody wants to be the one holding the match when things go up in smoke.
