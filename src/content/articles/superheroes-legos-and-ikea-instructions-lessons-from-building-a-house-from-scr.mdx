Building a house from the ground up sounds like a job for a superhero—someone focused, resilient, and ready for anything. But as I quickly learned, even superheroes need a plan, clear instructions, and a lot of patience (think assembling IKEA furniture without the manual). From finding land to managing budgets and endless design choices, this project became a true test of my leadership, teamwork, and handling life’s surprises. All relatable whether you’re managing a team, leading a new project, or juggling everyday life.

## Expect the unexpected costs

Imagine starting a Lego set with your kids, only to find half the pieces are missing and the replacements are twice the price. That’s what my house build felt like.

Every seemingly small detail had a huge cost impact. Big projects are almost guaranteed to go over budget, no matter how well you plan. Strong leaders set the tone by preparing the team early for inevitable surprises, so no one is blindsided. By managing expectations and communicating openly, you build trust and avoid “budget shock” down the line.

Saying "I don't know" isn't a sign of poor leadership. It is transparency, and it helps avoid the perception that important details are being hidden or missed.

## Align people from the start

It seems logical: find land, get plans, find a builder to use those plans, and then boom. House.

In my mind, the architect and builder would work together like Batman and Robin. But it quickly turned into Batman vs. Superman, with me playing referee. As a leader, you learn that alignment early on isn’t just a bonus. It is essential.

Ensuring everyone is on the same page, especially people with different perspectives, is what keeps a team moving in the right direction. Whether it’s a construction project or a team initiative, clear alignment at the start saves you from endless conflict resolution down the road.

## The small details will get you

If you’ve ever stepped on a Lego block, you know small things can hurt. Choosing every detail for the house—lighting, tiles, fixtures—felt like stepping on Legos daily. Each decision added up quickly, and without enough guidance, it became overwhelming. Leaders know that details matter, but a great experience I saw was to be challenged on something I thought was fairly "standard."

What kind of light?

- What color and style?
- Indoor or outdoor?
- Built-in LED or bulb?
- Dimmable?
- One switch or two?

Those questions are just for a light! Imagine all the other aspects. Encourage your team to bring up small issues early, and make it safe for them to ask questions. Clarity on details prevents stress later and keeps the team aligned with the vision.

> Strong leaders do not hide from the hard days. They normalize stress and create room to work through it.

## Stress relief is not just powering through

I try to lead with a "can do" attitude. However, big projects are stressful, and sometimes, I just needed to vent. Strong leaders don’t hide from the hard days; they normalize stress and create space for it.

By acknowledging that pressure affects everyone, leaders keep the team engaged and resilient. I put a lot of pressure on myself, especially having to look for a new role on top of everything. I got worn down and needed to ask for help. Stress management isn’t just a personal habit. It is a way of leading that lets the team know it’s okay to be human.

## Reflection is part of leading well

One thing Agile teams get right is the retrospective, and it’s valuable beyond product development. Building a house taught me that reflection is essential to effective leadership. It’s okay to admit you don’t have all the answers. Empowering your team to help find them builds ownership and trust.

Every project is a chance to learn, so keep adjusting and improving as you go. Great leaders treat every challenge as a lesson that makes the team stronger. Would I build a house from scratch again? I hope not. But if I had to, I know what I would do differently.
